## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored, not interchangeable: the state machine *is* the design. The visual and emotional layer is still a competent utility shell wrapped around it.**

No unrelated dev tool could ship `groups.ts` + `GroupPill.tsx` + Home's master sub-line unchanged, because the composition is built on a distinction most products refuse to make: intent versus observable flow, rendered as switch-versus-pill on one row, allowed to disagree. Five honest states where a category-typical app ships two and lies in the third. The `config file` / `proxy` chips with the mono host beneath each member name expose the mechanism instead of hiding it, which is what "credentials are the product" actually requires.

Where it is generic: the room is a list of cards with a switch on the right. Home, Settings and GroupDetail are one structural template, and the template is the 2019 macOS-menubar-utility default. The four takeovers are now one component with a 56px tile, a 17px heading and two stacked buttons, four times, with the glyph as the only differentiator, and two of the four use the same `shieldCheck`. ## What's Working

**`desired` vs `routed`, rendered as switch versus pill.** The whole product in one invariant: the switch is what you asked for, the pill is what is happening, and they may disagree. It eliminates a class of destructive-toggle bug by construction, and `memberPillLabel` / `groupPillLabel` being exported so the sr-only text and the visible pill cannot drift is what makes the honesty durable rather than careful.

**The header pill refuses to lie and everything downstream honours it.** Green means traffic is flowing, never "the engine is up", and `partial` outranks the count so "certificate not trusted" is never demoted to a bare "Nothing routing". For a product whose failure signal is silent drift, this is the most load-bearing correctness decision in the UI.

**`classifyError` names the button the user pressed.** Switch contexts get "Flip the Routing switch again", a 401 sends an OAuth user to sign-in and a key user to the key field, the vault is named per platform, and the raw payload has a copy button so it never has to be hand-selected in a 360px window. Error copy referencing the user's screen rather than the developer's vocabulary is rare.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] "Routing on, nothing routing" is an undesigned dead end

**Why it matters:** with `proxyOn && routedCount === 0 && stuckCount === 0` the screen shows a blue master switch, "On · nothing enabled yet", three grey pills, and no call to action. The launch-at-login tip may still render, advising the user to preserve a routing state that is routing nothing. This is the exact shape of the product's stated failure mode, silent drift to zero, and it is the one Home state with no next step, while the less consequential empty-ledger case gets a friendly card. I made this state truthful last round and left it without a remedy.

**Fix:** give it the same card treatment the empty state gets, with a one-line prompt near the ledger heading, and suppress the launch-at-login tip unless `routedCount > 0`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] `ErrorBoundary` is a stack trace with no exit and no traffic status

**Why it matters:** verified at `ErrorBoundary.tsx:33-46`. A render throw yields a 17px heading, one sentence, and then `error.name`, the full JS stack and the React component stack in an 11.5px mono `<pre>` — with no Reload, no Quit, and no statement about routing. This is the app that owns the machine's HTTPS proxy settings: a user here cannot tell whether their traffic is still flowing, cannot recover, and cannot quit, and the tray's Quit path routes through this same webview for its confirm.

**Fix:** reuse the `Takeover` shape. Heading "Gate Connect hit an error", one honest line about routing state, a Reload primary that re-mounts, and the stack collapsed behind the `<details>` + Copy-details pattern `ErrorNote` already has. While there, `rounded-lg` on that `<pre>` is the one off-scale radius in the app's own components.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] The header's gateway identifier truncates, and member names truncate in exactly the failing states

Two instances of one pattern, which is why they belong together.

**Why it matters:** the header host, carried over unfixed from last round, measures 176px against a slot that "Needs trust" and "Nothing to route" both squeeze below it; `gateway-staging.constellationgate.ai` at 227px truncates in every state, which is precisely the dev-mode audience that needs to know its environment. New this round, and the same shape: on GroupDetail the member name is `truncate text-[13px] font-medium` (`GroupDetail.tsx:220`) sharing its row with pill and switch, and "Claude Desktop / Cowork" at 157px renders as "Claude Desktop / C…" whenever the pill is wide — verified in the `master-off` and `needs-trust` states. Both are cases where an earlier fix moved the *host* to its own line for exactly this reason and left the more important string exposed to the same squeeze. Both truncate hardest when something is wrong.

**Fix:** the remedy `Settings.tsx:351` already uses. Full-width line for the header host under the wordmark/pill row; name on its own line above the pill/switch row on GroupDetail, or cap the pill and let it truncate instead of the name.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P2] The stale-address banner reports a broken state and offers no remedy

**Why it matters:** `banner === "stale"` says "Gate's local address changed. Close your apps; they reconnect the next time you open them." with only a dismiss X, while the change banner beside it, explicitly ranked lower, offers "Close them…" wired to the routing takeover. Stale is the state where the user's tools are broken *right now*, and it is the one making them do manual work the app can already do via `closeRunningAgents`.

**Fix:** add the same `onCloseAgents` action to the stale banner, keeping the X for people who prefer to close things themselves.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

### [P3] Three takeovers, two grammars for the safe exit

**Why it matters:** `RoutingChangeNotice` renders Cancel as a full-width secondary button and its own comment explains why, so the safe option is its equal. `QuitConfirm` and `UpdateTakeover` render Cancel and Later as bare 12.5px ink-3 text links — and in both, that link is what `initialFocus` targets, so the focused default is the least visible control on the panel. Quit is the highest-stakes of the three and it is the one where the safe choice was demoted. Consolidating the shell earlier made the geometry uniform and left this divergence untouched inside it.

**Fix:** promote Cancel to `variant="secondary" full` in `QuitConfirm`; three stacked buttons is fine at 360px. `UpdateTakeover` can keep the link, since "Later" is a deferral rather than a safety exit, but that should be a stated decision rather than an accident.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

## Questions to Consider

1. **If credentials are the product, why is the credential the smallest type in the room?** What would Home look like if the keychain line were a 13px row showing the key's prefix (`sk-gw-7Qx4••••`) rather than a 10.5px footnote: the key on its hook where you can see it, as DESIGN.md already asks for.
2. **Should `stuckCount > 0` demote the header pill to partial?** Home's pill can be green while a tool is in a hard error state. The ledger has an error vocabulary the home screen never uses. Or does that over-report and train people to ignore amber?
3. **Is a switch that knowingly does part of what it says still an honest control?** The family switch silently declines drifted members. Given how much of this product's identity rests on not overstating, indeterminate plus "1 left alone" may be the more consistent answer.
4. **If a user sees exactly two takeovers in their lifetime, should they look like the same kind of event?** Consolidating the shell was right for consistency and made the quit warning and the sign-in offer structurally identical, two of them sharing a glyph.
5. **Escape exits Settings and Group but not the popover.** On macOS, Escape on a menubar popover means dismiss. Is the keyboard user's mental model being served, or is the app teaching a private convention?
