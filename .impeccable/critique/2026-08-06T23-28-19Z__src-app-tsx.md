## Design Specificity Verdict

**The sentences are authored for this product. The shapes are not, and round 15
moved the one irreplaceable composition off the screen the product is judged on.**

**LLM assessment.** What could not be lifted into another product: the
intent/reality split rendered as switch-versus-pill, where `desired` drives the
switch and `routed` drives the pill and they are *allowed to disagree* (with
`aria-describedby` wiring the pill's sentence onto the switch, so a screen reader
hears "on" **and** "Needs trust" from one control); `memberPillLabel`'s
vocabulary ("Waiting on routing", "Needs trust", "Set up elsewhere");
the credential strip named per platform on every screen *including FirstRun where
no credential exists yet*; `QuitConfirm`'s third door, which exists only because
config files outlive the process; `ErrorBoundary` asking the backend whether
traffic is still routing *before* showing the error, with `unknown` as a
first-class state that never rounds to "off"; and `noticeFor`'s `"pending"`, a
banner that refuses to say "close your apps" when nothing is running.

Category furniture: Home's composition (icon-tile / title / sub-line / switch,
twice, then a title / sub-line / chevron door) is the Tailscale-1Password
settings-row pattern unmodified. Swap five strings and it is a VPN client.
Settings, FirstRun, the empty state and the header are all stock.

The structural move of round 15 was evacuating the ledger from Home to a `Routes`
panel. The ledger row *is* the irreplaceable artifact. PRODUCT.md principle 2
reads "The integrations list is home; each row's status pill is the most
important pixel on screen." Measured, the most important pixel is not on the
screen, and a third of the screen is empty. The design is specific in its
sentences and generic in its shapes, which is the rarer and more recoverable of
the two failure modes.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs --json src index.html tailwind.config.ts`
exited 2 with **8 findings, all 8 matched `ignore.md`** by file + value, so the
headline count is **0**. Rules seen: `design-system-color` (6),
`design-system-font-size` (1), `overused-font` (1). No `ignore.md` entry is
stale, and every recorded line number (35/116/120, 219, 159, 188/192, 56) is
still exact. The temporary harness files produced zero findings.

The live-DOM detector added 46 unique findings across 15 views, but they
collapse rather than accumulate: 29 were the detector reading **its own overlay
labels**; 12 `text-occlusion` hits are the by-design quit takeover covering an
`aria-hidden` Home; `flat-type-hierarchy`'s "1.7:1, top size 18px" is contaminated
by the injected banner's own 18px text (real ramps top out at 16-17px). Of what
remains, 24 instances are `tiny-text` / `undersized-ui-text` at 10.5px, which is
**on the documented DESIGN.md ramp** and was correctly not flagged by the CLI
rule, so those rules disagree with the sanctioned ramp rather than catching
drift. Mono identifiers at 10.5px are intentional and fine. The one place it is
not fine is where 10.5px ink-4 carries two sentences of instruction *and* fails
contrast, which is priority issue 3 below. **One live finding survives as a real
defect, and both assessments found it independently by different methods.**

## Overall Impression

This is a careful, unusually self-aware codebase whose comments frequently
diagnose their own screen better than a reviewer would, and whose invisible
layers (error prevention, focus management, accessible names, reduced motion) are
in the top few percent of anything at this size. The failure is not craft, it is
composition: the product's stated thesis is that the status pill is the most
important pixel, and round 15 removed every per-tool pill from the home screen
into a panel, leaving a third of Home empty with nothing scrolling. Meanwhile the
pill, wherever it does appear, is the faintest object on its row while the switch
beside it is the loudest.

**The single biggest opportunity:** put status back on Home. The vertical budget
already exists (160.8px free plus the door's 60.6px), so this costs no scroll and
no new navigation. It would fix the specificity verdict, the emptiness, the
recognition heuristic and Morgan's whole path at once.

## What's Working

**1. Error prevention, earned by construction rather than by dialogs.** The
`desired`/`routed` split makes the switch-inverts-itself bug unrepresentable, and
the code comment records the real bug it came from (an enabled domain behind an
untrusted CA rendered off, so clicking it turned off the thing the user was
turning on). Takeovers carry `aria-hidden` **and** `pointer-events-none`, so a
click cannot reach a control the user cannot see. `noticeFor`'s `"pending"` state
exists purely so the app can decline to give advice that would be false. This is
the difference between a product that confirms and a product that cannot be made
to lie.

**2. An accessibility floor most shipped software does not clear.** Measured
across 122 interactive elements in 16 views: **zero** missing accessible names
(73 by `aria-label`, 49 by text), **every** focus stop painting a 2px indigo ring
and sitting inside the viewport, modal containment holding over 8 consecutive
Tabs without escaping to the 5 controls behind, and reduced motion verified live
via `document.getAnimations()` (260ms running -> 0.01ms finished under emulation),
not inferred from CSS. The switch off-track is `#868c9e` at 3.36:1 rather than
the palette's `#d4d7e3` at 1.44:1, a deliberate SC 1.4.11 choice with the
reasoning written down. And the switch's 38x22 track carries
`before:-inset-y-1` to make the **hit area 38x30**, with a comment that does the
SC 2.5.8 exception analysis properly, noting that the sibling `absolute inset-0`
row button defeats the Spacing exception.

**3. The unglamorous screens carry the personality best.** `ErrorBoundary` leads
with "Your traffic is still routing through Gate" instead of a stack trace.
`QuitConfirm` names the exact tools with a correct Oxford join, offers a third
door, and puts focus on Cancel because "Enter on an unread panel should not
decide how". Round 14's chevron complaint is genuinely fixed: three depths now
have three affordance vocabularies (stroked chevron navigates, filled caret
expands, the word "Details" at the deepest level, so what the eye reads and what
a screen reader hears are the same string).

## Priority Issues

### [P1] Home no longer carries the pixel the product calls most important, and a third of it is empty

**What.** Measured: on `home-on`, `home-off`, `home-error` and `home-long`,
content ends at y=326.3 in a 487px scroll viewport, leaving **160.8px empty
(33.0%)** with `scrollHeight - clientHeight = 0`, so nothing is hidden below.
Home carries zero per-tool pills; its only pill is the global header one, which
restates the card sub-line 40px beneath it. The ledger, one navigation away, is
itself **38.7% empty** *with all four families rendering*. FirstRun is 42.1%.

**Round 14's numbers were wrong and are now corrected.** "41% empty" on Home is
reproducible only by stopping at the card bottom (y=290) and ignoring the "Gate
dashboard" button below it: (487-290)/487 = 40.5%. The true figure is 33.0%.
"64% empty" on the ledger requires content bottom at 175px, which is exactly
row 2's bottom, i.e. a **two-row** ledger; the current build renders four. That
figure was stale rather than wrong at the time.

**Why it matters.** PRODUCT.md principle 2 makes the integrations list the home
screen and the row pill the most important pixel; principle 3 says optimise for
staying, not starting. The mid-task user's question is "which of my tools is
broken", and answering it now costs a navigation plus a memory. The room that was
declared too small to hold a list is a third empty in its most common state.

**Fix.** Put compact rows back and keep the door as the way to the fine grain.
The budget is exact: 160.8px free plus the door's own 60.6px = **~221px without
introducing a scroll**. Current ledger rows are 61.4px (4 rows = 245.6px, does
not fit); a Home row carrying name + pill only, no switch and no caret, at 48px
gives 4 rows = 192px with ~29px spare. Leave the switches on the `Routes` panel.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

### [P1] Intent is 5.4x louder than reality on every ledger row

**What.** Measured on the expanded ledger: the switch's on-track is **5.98:1**
against white and 5.68:1 against the expanded row's fill. Every status pill's
wash is **1.09-1.16:1** against the row it sits on. Ratio: **5.39x**. The pill's
*text* passes AA comfortably (4.83:1 worst case), so this is not a colour-contrast
failure. The capsule is functionally invisible as an object.

**Why it matters.** The product declares the pill the most important pixel and
the switch a mere control; the eye receives the opposite instruction. `groups.ts`
already concedes the problem in a comment ("intent is one saturated indigo
object, so reality has to speak in more than one place to hold its own") and
compensated with ink colour on the exception line, which is the right instinct
applied to the wrong element.

**Fix.** Give the pill the Seam stack so the capsule reads at >=3:1 as a shape
(DESIGN.md forbids solid 1px borders, not shadow seams, and every other elevated
surface already wears one), or desaturate the on-track. The switch does not need
to be the most saturated object on a screen where it reports the least
interesting fact.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P1] The one machine-wide setting is the only text in the app that fails AA

**What.** "Sets `HTTPS_PROXY` for your whole shell, so OpenCode and other
terminal tools route too" renders at 10.5px in ink-4 `#7a7f93`, measured
**3.97:1** on white against a 4.5:1 requirement. It fails in all nine Home
states. Both assessments found this independently by different methods (ancestor
walk with alpha compositing; live-DOM detector) and returned the same element and
the same ratio. It is 22 failing instances out of 311 text runs measured, and the
only failing pair in the app.

**Why it matters.** Three compounding reasons. It is the only control in the
popover that changes something outside the AI tools: it reaches `git`, `curl` and
every process in the shell. DESIGN.md explicitly scopes ink-4 to "placeholders,
muted icons, and incidental mono identifiers only; never sentence copy or
labels", and this is two sentences of instruction. And `OAuthOffer.tsx` already
carries the comment "ink-3, not ink-4: DESIGN.md names ink-3 as the smallest ink
that may carry real text, and ink-4 measures 4.0:1 on white" - the team audited
this exact question, wrote down the answer, and missed the one place where the
consequence is a machine-wide environment change.

**Fix.** ink-3 (`#55596f`, measured 6.90:1). One token swap.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

### [P2] The sub-switch is visually equal to the master, and paints live when it cannot be

**What.** Measured: master switch at (294, 102.3), 38x22, indigo 5.98:1.
`Command-line tools` switch at (294, 168.8), 38x22, indigo 5.98:1. Same size,
same colour, same x, no indent, 66.5px apart, distinguished only by a 1.5px
font-size difference. In `home-off` the master is off, the card reads "Off ·
5 waiting", and the sub-switch is still saturated indigo.

**Why it matters.** This is precisely the intent-versus-reality problem the
ledger solved with `desired`/`routed` and a pill, left unsolved on the home
screen, in the app whose entire thesis is that its status is truthful. A channel
that cannot be live is painted in the colour DESIGN.md reserves for live state.
And the visual equality means a user cannot tell from geometry that one switch is
a sub-setting of the other rather than its peer.

**Fix.** Two changes: indent the sub-row to the text column (or drop it to a
28x16 track) so the hierarchy is visible without reading; and render it not-live
when `!proxyOn`, reusing `memberPillLabel`'s existing "Waiting on routing" string
so the app has one vocabulary for this condition instead of two.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] Two round-14 carryovers with no code behind either: `Agent harnesses`, and no documentation at all

**What.** `groups.ts:171` sets `name: "Agent harnesses"`, which surfaces both as
a ledger family name and inside Home's door subtext ("Claude, OpenAI, OpenRouter,
Agent harnesses"). Separately, verified three independent ways: the entire app
contains exactly **two** external URLs, `GATE_DASHBOARD_URL` and
`GATE_API_KEYS_URL`. There is no documentation link anywhere. Settings' "Help"
section contains "Replay tour" and "Dev mode".

**Why it matters.** "Agent harnesses" is the label on
`filter(t => !claimed.has(t.slug))` - literally "whatever the catalog didn't
claim". PRODUCT.md's positioning says "the UI's nouns are tools and apps", and no
user calls OpenCode a harness; it is the one word on Home a first-timer cannot map
to anything they installed. And a product that installs a local CA, runs a MITM
proxy and writes to the OS keychain, offering no documentation route, has a "Help"
section with no help in it.

**Fix.** Rename to something a user would say ("Other tools"), or dissolve the
group and list OpenCode, OpenClaw and Hermes as plain members; the `switchLabel`
grammar already handles the lowercase common-noun case either way. Add a docs
link under Settings > Help beside Replay tour.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Emotional Journey

Reassurance genuinely arrives in four places: FirstRun's "Credentials live in
your keychain" printed while the store is still empty (promise before ask, the
best beat in the product and it costs nothing); the quit panel; the crash screen;
and the errored-member wash that admits what Gate Connect *cannot* tell.

The valleys are concentrated where the product is judged. `home-error` is the
deepest: the user opens the popover *because* something stopped working, and gets
an amber pill, a count, a red sentence with no remedy, and 160.8px of white space
below it, with the red text rendered as body copy inside a button so nothing
signals it is the way in. Trusting a CA has two different emotional shapes
depending on which door you came through: one click and an explanation from Home,
three clicks and no remedy-at-that-level from the ledger. And on the healthy
Home, the largest, most saturated interactive text (`Gate dashboard`, 142.8x33.8,
14.5px indigo) points *out* of the product, so the final beat of the daily visit
is the exit.

Round 14's "the loudest element on Home is a dismissible banner" is **improved,
not fixed**: the banner moved below the routing card and got quieter, but in
`home-stale` and `home-change` it is still the only filled region on the screen.

## Questions to Consider

1. **If Home's job is "is anything wrong?", why is the answer a chevron?** What
   if the door *were* the exception - the failed tool's name, its pill, and a
   retry, rendered as a row - and the full inventory lived only behind it?
2. **You split intent from reality on every row except the one switch that
   rewrites the user's whole shell.** What is `HTTPS_PROXY` actually doing right
   now, and why is that the one channel with no pill?
3. **The pill is declared the most important pixel and measures 1.1:1 against its
   own row while the switch beside it measures 5.9:1.** Would you rather make
   reality louder or intent quieter? The second is one token and costs nothing.
4. **A third of the primary screen is empty in the healthy state and nothing
   scrolls.** What goes there if you are forbidden from adding a navigation? If
   the honest answer is "the ledger", round 15's structural move is worth
   reverting rather than defending.
