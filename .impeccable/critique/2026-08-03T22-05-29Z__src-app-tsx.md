## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored for this product in its state model and copy. Category-interchangeable in its composition and motion.**

**LLM assessment.** What could not be lifted into another dev tool is the state model. `src/lib/groups.ts` separates `desired` (what the user asked for) from `routed` (what is actually carrying traffic) and renders them as two different controls in the same row: the switch reports intent, the pill reports flow. That is why `memberPillLabel` can produce "Waiting on routing", a state most products would round to "off" or lie about as "on". The five-value `MemberAttention` union and `noticeFor(turnedOn, engineRunning, engineWasRunning)` in `App.tsx:125` encode truths no neighbouring app has to model. The copy is likewise unforgeable: "Codex has a Gate setup written outside this app. The group switch leaves it alone" (`GroupDetail.tsx:181`) is a sentence only this product can write.

What could ship unchanged in any Electron menubar utility is the shell. Home is a sticky header with a wordmark and gear, a hero card with icon tile and switch, a SectionLabel, and a hairline-divided list of rows with trailing chevrons. That is the Bartender / Shottr / Rectangle layout. The icons are verbatim Lucide geometry. Nothing in the composition says "gatehouse": no threshold, no ledger rule, no sense of a door with a keeper behind it. The north star lands in exactly two places, the pinned footer key line and the `#f6ffe3` highlight banner, and both are used well.

Does the popover make the user feel where the key lives? Partially, and it fails at the moment that matters most. The pinned footer (`App.tsx:1087`) is an excellent always-on answer, correctly per-platform via `secretStoreName`. But it is gated on `account && (has_api_key || signed_in)`, so it does not render on FirstRun, the one screen where a live `sk-gw-` secret is being pasted. Verified: `credentialStore` is `null` there (`App.tsx:1008`), and `Input` (`gc/ui.tsx:157`) spreads `...rest` but FirstRun passes no `type`, so the secret renders as 13px plaintext. Meanwhile Settings masks the stored key and puts a confirm panel in front of revealing its first eight characters. The credential hygiene is inverted: maximum ceremony around a stored prefix, none around the live secret.

Structural sameness: all four takeovers (QuitConfirm, RoutingChangeNotice, OAuthOffer, UpdatePanel) carry a byte-for-byte identical 90-character class string and hand-rolled focus-trap wiring. Consistency is correct here, but it is literal consistency; no one extracted the primitive.

Missed character: the ledger has no history and no evidence. A user who opens the popover on day 30 sees the same screen as day 1, with no proof anything ever flowed. The success metric is sustained routing, and nothing on screen is a receipt.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs` over `src/`, `index.html`, and `tailwind.config.ts` exits 2 with **7 raw findings, all 7 matched by `.impeccable/critique/ignore.md`, leaving 0 net.** Breakdown: `design-system-color` x5 (three `#000` mask-gradient alpha stops in `Onboarding.tsx`, two `oklch(0.165 0 0)` inside `color-mix()` shadow strings in `tailwind.config.ts`), `design-system-font-size` x1 (the 27px tour heading, which renders in the separate larger window), `overused-font` x1 (Geist in `index.html:56`, the locked product face). Two of these are genuine detector false positives independent of the ignore file: the `oklch(0.165 0 0)` hits are `ink.800` (defined at `tailwind.config.ts:71`, never flagged) and are only ever consumed as a seam alpha, but the detector's alpha exemption parses the bare literal rather than the enclosing `color-mix()`. Housekeeping: `ignore.md` records those two at lines 186/190; they are now at 188/192. Matching is by file plus value so the drop still works, but the navigation aid is stale.

**Visual overlays.** None. No browser was connected, so injection was never attempted and no user-visible overlay exists. This is the fallback signal, not a pass.

The detector and the design review agree on nothing, which is itself the finding: the token layer is disciplined and the mechanical surface is clean. Every issue below is a judgment call the detector structurally cannot see.

## Overall Impression

This is a careful app that has been worked on hard, and the work shows in the places that are hardest: partial states, error copy, and the suppression clauses that keep three identical buttons off the screen at once. QuitConfirm is the best screen in the product and one of the better confirm dialogs in any menubar app.

The single biggest opportunity is that the app's stated first principle has a hole in it exactly where a secret changes hands. Everything else is second-order. After that, the pill that can go green over zero routed tools is the thing most likely to cost a user real money quietly.

## What's Working

**Intent and flow are separate data, and they render as separate controls.** `GroupMember.desired` vs `GroupMember.routed` (`groups.ts:41`) is the spine of the UI. It is why a domain enabled behind an untrusted CA shows a switch that is on next to a pill that says "Needs trust", instead of a switch that lies about being off and inverts the user's next click. The in-code comment records that this bug existed and was fixed by the split. This is what lets the app be honest about half-states, which is the entire basis of the reassurance the product sells.

**Remedies travel with problems, including the suppression clause.** `needs-trust` gets a "Trust certificate" button inside the expanded member row. `master-off` gets "Turn on routing", and it is suppressed when the group banner already offers the same action (`GroupDetail.tsx:301`), because two expanded members would otherwise put three copies of one action on screen. The `pending` notice offers "Turn on routing" instead of "Close them" because there is nothing running to close. Getting the base case right is common; getting the suppression clause right is not.

**The pinned footer credential line.** One 10.5px row, key icon plus "Key in your keychain" / "Session in Credential Manager", routed through `secretStoreName(platform)` so it never names the wrong vault. Pinned outside the scroll container, so it costs Home zero height and appears on every screen. The core promise delivered as ambient furniture rather than a claim someone has to go find.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] The key-entry moment says nothing about where the key goes, and shows it in plaintext

**Why it matters:** This is the app's reason to exist. PRODUCT.md principle 1 is that every screen should make the user feel where the key lives. The one screen handling a live secret is the one screen that does not. Verified: `FirstRun.tsx:167` renders the `sk-gw-` field with no `type`, so it defaults to text; `credentialStore` is `null` on FirstRun because no account exists yet (`App.tsx:1008`), so the footer reassurance is suppressed; the same holds for Settings' Replace-key field. The asymmetry is stark on inspection, since revealing the first eight characters of a *stored* key requires clearing a confirm panel (`Settings.tsx:460`). A user pasting a production gateway key on a shared screen gets neither protection nor reassurance.

**Fix:** (a) Add a line under the key input in both FirstRun and Settings: "Saved to {secretStoreName(platform)}. Config files get the gateway URL, never the key." (b) Add an optional `secret` prop to `Input` that sets `type="password"` with a reveal toggle in the existing `trailing` slot; the eye/eyeOff icons already exist. (c) Render the footer credential line on FirstRun in a forward-looking voice ("Your key will live in your keychain") rather than suppressing it.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The header pill goes green while nothing is routing

**Why it matters:** Verified at `Home.tsx:137`: `pill={proxyOn && routableCount > 0 ? (partial ? "partial" : "connected") : "idle"}`. With routing on and zero members enabled this resolves to `connected`, so a green wash and "Routing on" sit directly above a master sub-line reading "On · nothing enabled yet" and three grey "Not routed" rows. Three surfaces, two verdicts. Green is the only signal a low-attention, mid-task user reads; they will close the popover believing their traffic flows through Gate, and the failure is silent until they notice their bill or their logs. DESIGN.md: pills report system state truthfully, they are never decorative.

**Fix:** The escape hatch already exists and was simply not applied to this branch. `pillLabel` currently handles only `routableCount === 0` ("Nothing to route"). Add: when `proxyOn && routableCount > 0 && routedCount === 0`, pass `pill="idle"` with `pillLabel="Nothing routing"`. Cover it in `Home.test.tsx`, which tests the empty-ledger case but not this one.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] Settings is seven undifferentiated text actions in four colours, with the destructive one at peer weight

**Why it matters:** Replace key (`ink-2`), Switch to Constellation sign-in (`accent`), Switch organization (`accent`), Sign out (`ink-3`), Replay tour (`ink-3`), Dev mode (`ink-3`), Reset Gate Connect (`error-deep`) are all `12.5px font-medium inline-flex gap-1.5` with a 14px leading icon. Dev mode and Reset Gate Connect share one `justify-between` flex row (`Settings.tsx:661`), so a debug toggle and an account-destroying action are literal visual siblings. Settings is where a user goes mid-incident, and seven equal-weight actions means rescanning all seven every visit. The Reset confirm is currently doing structural work that visual hierarchy should be doing.

**Fix:** One primary per section, everything else demoted. Move Replay tour and Dev mode into the tray right-click menu or a single overflow. Give Reset its own bottom-anchored strip with a hairline above and nothing beside it. Cut the inline-action colour vocabulary from four to two: `accent` for the one encouraged action per section, `ink-3` for the rest, `error-deep` reserved for Reset alone.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### [P2] The documented motion grammar is not implemented for the screens it names

**Why it matters:** DESIGN.md and CLAUDE.md both promise that secondary surfaces slide in as full-popover panels on a 260ms directional curve. Verified: `gc-panel-in` appears only in `QuitConfirm`, `RoutingChangeNotice`, `OAuthOffer`, and `UpdatePanel`; `ob-slide-in-fwd`/`-back` appear only in `Onboarding.tsx`, which is a separate window. `App.tsx` swaps `body` with no wrapper, so Home to Settings, Home to GroupDetail, back, and FirstRun to Success are all hard cuts. In a 360px one-room popover, direction is the only navigational metaphor available; without it, back and forward are indistinguishable and the user has no spatial model of where Settings sits. It is also the most visible unfulfilled promise in the design docs, which makes the spec less trustworthy to whoever implements against it next.

**Fix:** Wrap `{body}` in a keyed container and apply `ob-slide-in-fwd` / `ob-slide-in-back` on a depth comparison (home = 0, settings/group = 1). The keyframes, the curve, and the `prefers-reduced-motion` collapse already exist in `index.css`. Roughly ten lines.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable animate`

### [P2] GroupDetail's identifier line is a dead zone that looks clickable

**Why it matters:** The member's stretch button is `absolute inset-0` of the *upper* flex row only (`GroupDetail.tsx:197-241`). The identifier line below it, carrying the `config file` / `proxy` chip and the host, is a sibling div marked `pointer-events-none` (line 250). When the row is expanded it takes `bg-gc-subtle`, so it reads as part of the same highlighted row. Roughly the bottom 40% of every member row is inert while looking identical to the active 60%. Aiming for the row and landing 8px low produces nothing, which reads as an unresponsive app. It also breaks the pattern set on Home, where the equivalent row is fully clickable.

**Fix:** Move the identifier line inside the container that carries the stretch button, or extend the button to `inset-0` of an outer wrapper covering both lines. The existing `pointer-events-none` on the content spans already handles click pass-through.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable polish`

## Cognitive Load

**4 of 8 checklist items fail (critical band), but three of the four are concentrated in Settings.** Home alone would fail 0 to 1.

- **Chunking: FAIL.** Settings' Account section is one undelimited run of seven items: gateway row, host line, key field, "Stored in your keychain", Replace key, Switch to Constellation sign-in, and a two-line blurb.
- **Visual hierarchy: FAIL.** Dev mode and Reset Gate Connect in one flex row at identical size and weight, differing only in colour.
- **Minimal choices: FAIL.** See decision points below.
- **Working memory: FAIL.** The Home to GroupDetail count handoff, plus a certificate story that starts on Home and finishes in Settings in different words.
- Passing: single focus (the Routing card is unambiguously primary), grouping (SectionLabel plus full-bleed dividers plus card seams do real work), one thing at a time (the takeover priority ladder at `App.tsx:1035` is explicit and correct), and progressive disclosure, which is exemplary: mechanism hidden until GroupDetail, "What's this?" collapsed, raw payloads behind `<details>`, the key form collapsed behind "Use an API key instead".

**Decision points above the 4-item working memory limit:**
- Settings in API-key mode: **12 controls**. OAuth mode: 11.
- Home, worst case: **13 targets** (routing switch, Trust, "What's this?", banner action, banner dismiss, launch-tip link, tip dismiss, three row buttons, three row switches, gear, dashboard link). Chunking mitigates it, but the transient chrome genuinely competes with the ledger.
- GroupDetail with two members expanded: 8+.
- FirstRun with the key form open: 5.

The takeovers all pass cleanly (QuitConfirm = 3 options, RoutingChangeNotice = 2 per step). They are the best-composed screens in the app.

## Emotional Journey

**Peak, achieved:** `Success.tsx`. The 46px hex mark with a green check ring, "You're connected", and the pinned footer already reading "Key in your keychain" underneath it. Two independent signals, one visual and one factual, arriving together.

**Peak, unexpected:** `QuitConfirm`. "Claude Code and Codex still route through Gate. If you quit now, they can't connect until Gate Connect runs again," followed by an offer to restore their own settings for the downtime. That is the reassuring gatekeeper fully realized: it knows what depends on it, says so without alarm, and offers to clean up after itself. Focus lands on Cancel, not on either quit path.

**Valley 1, the key paste.** Highest stakes, lowest reassurance. See P0.

**Valley 2, the loading screen.** `App.tsx:878` renders a static 40px hex and the wordmark. No `role="status"`, no text, no timeout, no error path. On macOS first launch the Keychain prompt sits over it. Every startup read is individually `.catch()`-ed to a default, so a partial backend failure produces a plausible-looking home screen rather than an honest one.

**Valley 3, the green pill over zero.** See P1.

**Valley 4, the half-failed group toggle.** "Couldn't connect 2 of 3: Codex, OpenCode" is good copy, but the two failing rows are not individually marked and the error note pushes the ledger further down. The naming was the hard part and it was done; the pointing was not.

**The end, for the daily user.** The success metric is sustained routing, so the real "end" is closing the popover after flipping a switch. The last thing the user reads is "Routing is on. Anything already open isn't routing through Gate yet." Honest and correct, but the closing impression is "your stuff isn't working yet", not "good, that's handled". There is no counterpart line for the state the user actually wants confirmed, which is that everything is fine and has been fine.

**Degraded states** are handled better than in most products of this kind: the "Needs trust" partial pill, the "Waiting on routing" master-off pill, and a certificate card that names the reason and carries the Trust button inline rather than burying it in the disclosure.

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (impatient power user).** No keyboard shortcut for the Routing switch, the single action he repeats forty times a week. Escape exits Settings and GroupDetail but does nothing on Home, so "open, act, dismiss" has no keyboard path and he must mouse to a 38x22px target. While `proxyBusy` is true, `[&_*]:!cursor-wait` slams wait cursors across the whole tree, so an admin prompt makes the popover feel frozen rather than working. After his first acknowledged takeover, the inline routing banner replaces it correctly, but that banner then persists until he clicks the X, on every toggle, forever.

**Riley (stress tester).** Kills the backend mid-session: `refreshState` catches every read into a default, so Home renders a confident "Nothing to route yet" empty state indistinguishable from a genuinely empty machine. Blocks the keychain: the loading screen hangs indefinitely with no text and no timeout. Triggers a partial group failure: gets the correctly named error, but neither failing row is marked and the note pushes the pills further below the fold.

**Jordan (confused first-timer).** Skips the tour, lands on FirstRun, picks "Use an API key instead" because he already has one. Pastes it in plaintext, sees no mention of a keychain, completes. He now believes the app wrote his key somewhere, which is the exact anxiety the product exists to remove. On Home he flips the Routing master and nothing changes on the rows, because the master does not enable members. The sub-line says "On · nothing enabled yet" while the header pill says green "Routing on". He has no model for why "on" produced nothing.

**Priya (project-specific: the drifted-config veteran who hand-wrote `ANTHROPIC_BASE_URL` into her agent's own config before Gate Connect existed).** The highest-value convert, and the app nearly nails her. Her row shows "Set up elsewhere", the group banner explains the switch will skip her, and adopting requires a confirm promising her settings return on disconnect. But `groupSummary` drops the count in favour of the exception (`groups.ts:192`), so her row reads "Codex set up elsewhere" with no number and she cannot tell from Home whether the other members are routing. Meanwhile the master card counts her drifted tool in the `routableCount` denominator ("3 of 8 routing") while `stuckCount` reports it as needing attention, so the denominator sets a target the Home controls structurally cannot reach. She reads the app as broken when it is behaving as designed.

**Marco (project-specific: PRODUCT.md's CI and headless-machine API-key holdout).** `FirstRun.connectWithKey` calls `markOAuthOfferSeen()` so he is not nagged next launch, which is thoughtful. But in Settings, "Switch to Constellation sign-in" is the only `accent`-coloured control in the Account section, sitting directly above his own key. And when his key eventually 401s, `classifyError`'s unauthorized branch tells him to replace his Gate API key in Settings. That is correct for him. The same branch fires for an OAuth user on a revoked session and points them at a control that does not exist in OAuth mode.

## Minor Observations

- `classifyError` hardcodes "keychain" on every platform in one branch (`errors.ts:146`), then says "credential store" in the next sentence. Every other string in the app routes through `secretStoreName`.
- The 401 branch (`errors.ts:168`) is auth-mode-blind; it should branch on `account.auth_mode`. See Marco.
- `Home` receives `caTrusted={proxy?.ca_trusted ?? true}` (`App.tsx:968`) while GroupDetail and the two other call sites use `?? false` (`:704`, `:871`, `:943`). Impact is low, since a null `proxy` also means `proxyOn` is false and the trust surfaces stay hidden, but defaulting a security-relevant unknown to trusted is the wrong direction and the inconsistency invites a future bug.
- `aria-hidden` without `inert`: `App.tsx:1070` hides the background from AT while a takeover is up, but pointer events still reach it. Focus traps cover Tab; a mouse click on the obscured gear still fires.
- The `sr-only` live region on Home (`Home.tsx:147`) announces on every state change including ones the user did not cause, so a screen-reader user gets an unprompted announcement when the backend auto-enables at startup.
- Spaced hyphens as dashes. The em dash ban is honoured (zero in `src/`), but the substitute is " - ": "through Gate - right from the menu bar" (`FirstRun.tsx:124`). At 12.5px in Geist that reads as a hyphenation artefact. A comma serves better; the rule bans one character, not the punctuation function.
- `SectionLabel`'s `dense` prop is dead. Its own comment says it was tuned for a four-family ledger that cannot currently occur (`groups.ts:11`). No caller passes it.
- The raw-payload `<details>` has no copy button, on five error branches whose hint text says the details help when reporting.
- Four takeovers duplicate the same 90-character layout class string and the same `useFocusTrap` wiring. One `Takeover` primitive would stop the next one drifting.
- Housekeeping: `.impeccable/critique/ignore.md` records the two `tailwind.config.ts` exceptions at lines 186 and 190; they are now at 188 and 192.

## Questions to Consider

1. **What if the pill were a receipt instead of a claim?** The success metric is sustained daily routing, yet nothing in the popover proves traffic has ever flowed. "Routed · 12m ago" on a group row would do more for day-30 trust than every onboarding screen combined, and it would make the green pill impossible to lie with, because a pill that has to name a timestamp cannot be green over zero.

2. **Is "Routing" the right master, or an implementation detail leaking upward?** The master controls the proxy engine, which is why turning it on routes nothing. Every awkward state in the app (`master-off`, "Waiting on routing", the `pending` notice, the group banner, the suppression clause) exists to explain the gap between the master and the members. What would the screen look like if there were no master, and enabling any member started the engine implicitly?

3. **Why does the app need a Settings screen at all?** The gateway host is already in the header. Launch at login is a one-time decision that belongs in the tour or the tray menu. The certificate belongs on the card that already explains it on Home. Replay tour and Dev mode belong in the tray right-click menu. Reset belongs behind Dev mode. Settings holds seven things that each have a better home, and deleting it would fix three of the four cognitive-load failures.

4. **Should the popover ever go green?** The Gatehouse metaphor is a keeper who waves you through, not a dashboard that congratulates you. What if the resting state were ink-neutral and colour appeared only when something needed the user? Green-when-healthy is the enterprise-SaaS reflex the anti-references reject, and it is the mechanism by which the header pill currently lies.

5. **The key is masked when stored and naked when typed. Which of those is the honest design?** There is a defensible position that masking a secret the user just copied from their own clipboard is theatre. But the app cannot hold both positions at once: either the `sk-gw-` string deserves protection or it does not, and right now the ceremony is inversely proportional to the exposure.
