## Design Specificity Verdict

**Specific in its language, interchangeable in its composition.** I agree with A, and the tension is sharper than either assessment alone shows.

**LLM assessment.** The details are unmistakably this product. The `desired` vs `routed` split in `groups.ts:41-54` is a distinction almost no product needs and this one cannot survive without, and its comment records the destructive bug that conflating them caused: an enabled domain behind an untrusted cert rendered off, so clicking it turned off the setting the user was trying to turn on. `secretStoreName()` and `trustStoreName()` keep the secret vault and the CA vault distinct per platform. "Set up elsewhere" is a first-class state. These are load-bearing and they are authored.

The composition is not. Strip the strings from Home and it is stock settings-list furniture: 36px tile, title, sub-line, master switch, mono section label, then N rows of name / pill / switch / chevron. A VPN client or a backup agent would ship it unchanged. The evidence is the whitespace: 40 to 70% of the room is empty in every ordinary state, which is the signature of a layout inherited from a list that expects to grow. `groups.ts:9-13` says outright that the ledger is three rows and stays three rows. Meanwhile the product's first principle, make the user feel where the key lives, is served by a 10.5px line pinned to the bottom edge of the one screen with 370px to spare.

Second tell, and the sharpest thing in A's report: `PRODUCT.md` says the pill is "the most important pixel on the screen." On every captured row the switch beats it on size, saturation and motion. In the routing-off state the brightest element on the row is an indigo switch carrying a white confirmation check, sitting 40px from a grey pill reading "Not routed". The hierarchy contradicts the stated principle.

**Deterministic scan.** `detect.mjs` over `src`, `index.html`, `tailwind.config.ts`: **7 findings, all 7 dropped per `ignore.md`, 0 surviving.** B re-verified all seven at source and every `ignore.md` claim still holds, including the two `oklch(0.165 0 0)` seam literals in `tailwind.config.ts` and the three `#000` mask-image gradients in Onboarding. The ignore list is masking no drift.

B also found one thing the detector **missed**: `Onboarding.tsx:78` carries `drop-shadow-[0_14px_34px_rgba(20,38,102,0.5)]`. `rgb(20,38,102)` is off palette (nearest is gatehouse-navy `#002a5f` = `rgb(0,42,95)`) and unlike the `#000` mask literals it is actually painted. The detector flagged the masks on that same file and not this.

And it found things no static rule catches, by arithmetic:

- `text-[10px]` at `GroupDetail.tsx:261, 269, 271`, below the 10.5px ramp floor. These are the `config file` / `proxy` mechanism chips, that is, the most product-specific detail in the app is the one off the type scale.
- `ink-5` chevron at `UpdatePanel.tsx:233` measures **2.42:1** on white, failing the 3:1 non-text bar.
- `aria-hidden` on a subtree containing focusable controls at `App.tsx:1119`. The in-file comment says `inert` was wanted and React 18's DOM typings lack it. Paired with `pointer-events-none` and a focus trap, so it is a spec violation rather than a live trap.
- `gap-[3px]` and `pl-[25px]` in `PopHeader.tsx:22, 33`, off the 4px grid.
- The `IconButton` primitive (`ui.tsx:67`) does not enforce an accessible name. All 8 call sites pass one, so nothing unnamed ships today; the guard is latent, not present.

**Verified clean, and worth stating because these are the things that usually rot:** zero em dashes, en dashes or horizontal bars anywhere in `src/` (codepoint-exact). ink-4 and ink-5 fail 4.5:1 as text, and B grepped all 11 usages to confirm every one is a glyph, never sentence copy, exactly as DESIGN.md requires. DESIGN.md's switch-contrast rationale is true to the decimal: `#d4d7e3` is 1.44:1 and `#868c9e` is 3.36:1 on white, 3.19:1 on subtle. The `prefers-reduced-motion` collapse catches every one of the 42 transitions, both keyframe animations and the infinite spinner, with nothing escaping: no delays, no JS-driven motion, no inline transitions.

**Visual overlays.** None. No connected browser, so no injection was attempted and no overlay is visible to you. A's 31 screenshots are ordinary captures, not instrumented ones.

## Overall Impression

This is a well-made instrument with a badly proportioned room, and the gap between those two facts is now the whole story. The mechanical layer is in better shape than at any previous round: the detector is clean against the ignore list, the contrast contract is provably true, the motion contract is airtight, the two token categories that were drifting are converged. Every remaining problem is compositional or behavioural, which is exactly the class of problem that static tooling cannot see and that this round's screenshots finally exposed.

Two of those are P0, and both are the same shape: **the app is at its least legible precisely when something is wrong.** An errored tool looks like a tool you switched off. A destructive confirm arms itself off-screen so pressing Reset appears to do nothing. For a product whose entire pitch is "you can trust what this window tells you", failure states rendering as shrugs is the most expensive defect available.

The single biggest opportunity is not a fix, it is a recomposition. Home is a scrolling list holding three permanent rows in a 360x520 room, spending 40 to 70% of that room on nothing while the product's core promise gets 10.5px at the bottom edge. Every heuristic scoring 2 (status visibility, recall, efficiency) would improve if the space were spent on reality instead of reserved for growth that `groups.ts` says will not come.

## What's Working

1. **The intent-versus-flow split, carried consistently through the whole stack.** `groups.ts:41-54` splits `routed` (traffic is flowing) from `desired` (what the user asked for); `Home.tsx:475-483` and `GroupDetail.tsx:233-242` drive the switch from `desired` and the pill from `routed`; `GroupPill.tsx:46-55` names the four states only an individual member can be in. This works because it is the only honest rendering of a system where the setting and the traffic can legitimately disagree, and because the alternative was a bug that turned off the thing the user was trying to turn on.

2. **The header pill refuses to round up.** `Home.tsx:148-159` requires actual flow before green, with "Nothing routing" and "Nothing to route" overriding the default label. A's screenshot 05 proves it: routing on, nothing installed, pill grey. This works because green is the only signal a low-attention user takes in, and the product's entire value is that the green can be trusted. This was a P1 two rounds ago and it is genuinely fixed.

3. **Remedies travel with the problem.** The Trust button lives on the card that names the certificate as the blocker (`Home.tsx:295-306`), "Turn on routing" lives on the member row that says nothing is flowing (`GroupDetail.tsx:309-319`), and it replaces "Close them…" in the pending banner where closing apps would be busywork (`Home.tsx:366-373`). This works because it removes the navigate-back-to-fix-it round trip in a window where navigation is expensive.

4. **The contracts that are supposed to be invariant actually are.** Zero em dashes, every ink-4 and ink-5 use a glyph rather than text, the documented contrast numbers true to two decimals, and a reduced-motion collapse with no escape hatch. This is the part of a design system that quietly rots, and B could not find a hole in it.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] An errored tool is indistinguishable from a tool you switched off, under a green header

**What.** `GroupPill.tsx:13` returns only `Routed` / `Partly routed` / `Not routed`, and any family with `routed === 0` gets grey "Not routed" regardless of why. `Home.tsx:462` puts the reason in 11px `ink-2`. `Home.tsx:149` still flies green "Routing on" because `stuckCount` never demotes the header pill. The result: green pill, grey pill, "Codex failed" in 11px grey. With a production-length tool name that line truncates to "Codex Command Line Interfa…" and loses the verb, defeating the exception-first ordering `Home.tsx:452-456` was built to guarantee.

I verified this myself: `memberPillLabel`, eight lines below `groupPillLabel` in the same file, already has an `"Error"` state. The vocabulary exists at member level and was never lifted to family level.

**Why it matters.** This is the app's core failure mode for its own described user. `PRODUCT.md` says Morgan opens the popover mid-task because a tool stopped connecting. Home answers that question in the same greys it uses for "you turned this off", under a header still reading green. The one moment the product must be legible is the one where it is quietest.

**Fix.** Add an `"Error"` state to `groupPillLabel` and `GroupPill`, using the skin `MemberPill` already defines at `GroupPill.tsx:67` (`bg-gc-error-wash text-gc-ink-2`, `bg-gc-error-deep` dot). Make `stuckCount > 0` force the header pill to `partial`. Both are additive to vocabulary that already exists.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P0] The destructive Reset confirm renders off-screen, so pressing Reset looks like nothing happened

**What.** `Settings.tsx:734-754`. The trigger is the last element in the scroll container and the `ConfirmPanel` it arms is rendered after it. On an OAuth account the trigger is already clipped by the pinned footer, and clicking it produces a pixel-identical screen because the confirm mounts below the fold with nothing scrolling to it. Same shape at `confirmingUntrust` (`Settings.tsx:646`) and `confirmingServer` (`Settings.tsx:718`).

**Why it matters.** The user has pressed the control that wipes their account and the app appears inert. That is the exact setup for pressing it again. It also inverts the one dimension this app has been strongest on: every destructive act here has a named-consequence confirm, and this one is invisible at the moment it arms.

**Fix.** On mount, `ConfirmPanel` calls `scrollIntoView({ block: "nearest" })` on its own ref and moves focus to its Cancel button. One change in the shared component fixes all three sites.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The popover misses its own WCAG AA commitment in three measured places

Three findings, one root: `PRODUCT.md` commits to WCAG 2.1 AA and full keyboard operability inside the popover, and the fixed 360px window removes the escape hatches a normal page has.

**a. The key field has no perceptible focus state and no accessible name.** `ui.tsx:181` sets `outline-none` on the `<input>`; the wrapper's only focus feedback is `focus-within:shadow-border-hover`, a 6% to 9% hairline change. A and B computed this independently from source and agree: **1.065:1** and **1.07:1**. The global `button:focus-visible` ring at `index.css:26-29` does not match inputs. Separately, B found the input has `placeholder` only, no `aria-label`, `aria-labelledby`, or associated `<label>`, so it has no accessible name at all. This is the field where a live `sk-gw-` key is pasted. DESIGN.md's Inputs rule, "no glow, no accent ring", directly causes an SC 2.4.7 failure.

**b. Every right-hand control is unreachable at 200% zoom.** `App.tsx:1125` sets `overflow-x-hidden` on the sole scroll container, so at a 200%-zoom-equivalent viewport the header pill, the gear, every row switch and the Trust button are clipped with no horizontal scroll. In a fixed 360px window zoom is the only enlargement path, and it is unhandled.

**c. Backend-initiated state changes are announced to nobody.** `Home.tsx:170-178` gates the polite live region on `interacted`, so the startup auto-enable, the event that changes whether the user's traffic is routing, reaches no screen reader.

**Why it matters.** Not a hypothetical persona: the app's own stated target is AA on a white surface with no dark-mode fallback, and these are three ways it does not get there, in the credential field, at zoom, and in the announcement path.

**Fix.** Add `focus-within:outline focus-within:outline-2 focus-within:outline-offset-2 focus-within:outline-gc-accent` to the Field wrapper and give the input a name. Change `overflow-x-hidden` to `overflow-x-auto` as a floor; better is stacking each row's pill and switch under the name below a width threshold. Drop the `interacted` gate on the live region for backend-initiated flips.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable audit`

### [P1] ErrorBoundary is still a stack trace with no exit and no traffic status

**What.** `ErrorBoundary.tsx:33-46` renders a heading, one sentence, then `error.name`, `error.message`, the full `error.stack` and the component stack in a `<pre>`. No Reload. No Quit. No statement of whether traffic is still routing.

**Provenance note:** this is mine, not A's. It was a P1 last round, it is unfixed, and A read the file without surfacing it. I checked the source directly rather than relying on either agent.

**Why it matters.** This is the one screen that appears when everything else has failed, and it hands a developer a JS stack trace and no way out, in a window with no menu bar and no reload affordance. The unanswered question is the only one that matters at that moment: is my API traffic still flowing? The app knows.

**Fix.** Keep the stack behind the existing `Details` disclosure pattern (`ui.tsx:285`), lead with whether routing is still up, and give the panel a Reload and a Quit.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] First run ends by contradicting itself

**What.** `Success.tsx:31` passes `pill={proxyOn ? "connected" : "idle"}` and `PopHeader.tsx:50` renders `idle` as **"Routing off"**, directly above the `<h1>` "You're connected". I verified both lines. The copy patches over it with "One step left", but the pill is the loudest element on the screen and it disagrees with the headline. Compounding it, the success check at `Success.tsx:37` is white on solid `bg-gc-success`, which B measures at **2.10:1**, failing both SC 1.4.11 and DESIGN.md's own Wash-First Rule.

**Why it matters.** Peak-end rule. This is the last screen of onboarding, seen once by every user, and it is the app's single best opportunity to produce "good, that's handled". Instead it produces a headline and a status pill that contradict each other, with a success glyph that does not meet contrast.

**Fix.** Suppress the header pill on Success, or make the headline conditional on `proxyOn`. Move the check to the wash-plus-solid-glyph treatment the rest of the system uses.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

## Questions to Consider

1. If 40 to 70% of the room is empty in every ordinary state, and `groups.ts:9-13` says the ledger is three rows and stays three rows, why is Home a scrolling list at all? What would it look like composed for exactly three families and one master switch, with the recovered space spent on the proof the product says is its whole value: which credential, which org, what is on the wire?
2. The switch reports intent and the pill reports reality. On a 360px row at a glance the switch wins on size, saturation and motion, and it carries a confirmation check. What if the row's dominant element were reality, and intent were the small thing?
3. The gateway host is identical for every customer. The org is what gets billed and what the gateway rejects requests without. Should `PopHeader`'s mono sub-label be the org rather than the host?
4. Gate Connect can close tools but not start them, and a substantial share of the product's copy, three banner variants, a full takeover and the `ChangeNotice` state machine all exist to work around that one asymmetry. What would it cost to detect the relaunch instead, and delete the entire hint apparatus?
5. If sustained daily routing is the success metric and silent disconnects are the named failure signal, is a status surface that can quietly be hours stale the right shape, or does this want a heartbeat?
6. Three placements of the "Gate dashboard" link have each failed differently. Is the honest answer that it does not belong on Home at all?
